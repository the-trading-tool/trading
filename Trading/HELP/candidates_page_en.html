This page leads from the whole universe down to a shortlist: a handful of names worth a closer look in the Asset Viewer. The building blocks already existed, but scattered across five pages — market picture on the dashboard, rotation under Market, formula filters in the asset overview, signal in the own-trades tab. Here they form one chain, and every step shows how much it removes.

The list is a shortlist, not a buy order. It says which names are worth the analysis — not which to buy. That decision stays with the chart and with you.

The chain

The order is deliberate: the steps run from cheap to expensive. The broad filters work on the simulation database (one pass over every ticker), relative strength needs a price series per name, and the live signal path computes one name at a time.

Why two different filters?

The prefilter is deliberately not your buy formula. Two reasons:

  • The buy formula may use columns that exist only live — ovtEma9 or atc_mid_zero, for instance, are not in asset_simulation. Run as SQL against the database, it fails.
  • The chart markers are position-aware: a sell signal only applies while you hold. A plain column query cannot express that.

Hence a broader, database-capable prefilter here — and the authoritative check at the end, on the short list.

Prefilter errors read usefully: if the message names a missing column, that is exactly the answer — the column exists only live. Available are all columns of asset_simulation, such as overallValueTrend, ewo, ewo_ema, relvol_ratio, sup_support, sup_resistance, adx, rsi, fps_phase, plus the raw OHLC Open/High/Low/close.

Pitfalls

  • The most recent day is often incomplete. While the simulation run is still going, only a few hundred tickers exist for today. The page therefore takes the most recent row per ticker from the last ten days, not the most recent day overall.
  • Use the ISIN filter with care. Only about 70 % of tickers have an ISIN on file; a missing one usually means "not resolved yet" rather than "not tradable".
  • A run costs time (typically 25–40 seconds), almost entirely in the relative-strength step and the signal path. The result stays in the session until a setting changes — sorting and selecting in the table recomputes nothing.
  • A weakness-seeking prefilter and the sector comparison contradict each other. Hunting pullbacks with close < sup_support finds names currently behind their sector — the "beats its own sector" step then removes every one of them. Switch that step off for such filters.
  • Empty result? Usually the sector threshold is too high (in a weak market no sector qualifies) or the prefilter too narrow. The funnel shows which step did it.
