Key Data – metrics in detail

The "Key Data" tab bundles an asset's most important metrics from three sources: the in-house signal model (asset_simulation), the fundamentals (asset_info) and the local price history. Tiles only appear when data is available – indices, metals, ETFs and crypto therefore show fewer than single stocks (no valuation/balance/dividend).

Sector comparison (equities only)

Places the asset in relation to its GICS sector (its industry peers) – computed entirely locally. This reveals whether a metric is strong in absolute terms and versus peers.

MetricMeaningUse for your decision
SectorThe asset's GICS sector; the tooltip names the matching sector ETF (e.g. Technology → XLK).Industry context – defines the peer group for the comparisons below.
OVT rankPosition of the OVT among all names in the sector, e.g. 53/350 – rank 1 = highest OVT (best). The tooltip gives the share of the sector the value beats ("beats X %").Leader or laggard within its own sector? Rank 53/350 = top decile; a rank close to the total (e.g. 294/309) means it is a laggard. A high OVT counts for more when it also ranks well relative to peers.
vs {ETF} (3M)Relative strength: the asset's 3-month return minus the sector ETF's (delta in percentage points).Positive (green) = genuine alpha – the asset beats its own sector, not just the broad market.
P/E vs sectorDistance of the P/E to the sector median (in %).Negative (green) = cheaper than the industry. Puts an absolutely high valuation into perspective.
Margin vs sectorDistance of the net margin to the sector median (in percentage points).Positive (green) = more profitable than peers – a quality signal.

The sector aggregates (median, rank distribution) are cached per sector (once per hour) and reused for every name in the sector. The section only appears for equities that have a sector on file.

Data-driven display: when a metric is missing, its tile is hidden automatically. The signal (OVT, trends, TP/SL) comes primarily from the current-year model; for indices, metals, FX and crypto it automatically falls back to the all-time model (asset_simulation_all). The week/month arrows below OVT and Overall trend are omitted when there is too little history.
No single metric is a buy/sell reason on its own. Combine the signal (is the trend intact?), the valuation (not too expensive?), the quality/balance (solid?) and the risk (fits your risk tolerance?) – and always set a stop-loss.

Fundamentals – context rather than a single number

The “Fundamentals” tab answers a different question than “Key Data”: not how high a metric is, but how it compares – against the industry and against its own history. It only appears for equities with stored annual statements (asset_info.incomeSheet/balanceSheet, about 5,400 of 5,900 equities).

Four ranks in the header

Financial strength, growth, profitability and valuation are shown as 0–10. The value is the mean percentile of that group's metrics within the peer group – a genuine cross-section over the locally stored equities, not a rating taken from elsewhere. 100 (or 10) always means “best in the group”: for valuation metrics that is the cheapest, not the highest. A rank needs at least three usable metrics, a percentile at least ten comparable values.

Switching the peer group

At the top you can switch between industry (narrow) and sector (broad). For small industries the sector gives the more stable distribution. The header always names the group in use and its size.

Formulas per metric

The calculation behind every single metric is in the tab itself, at the very bottom under “📖 How the metrics are calculated”. That table is generated from the engine's metric registry and therefore cannot drift from the implementation – every metric lists its calculation, its source and its direction (whether “high” or “low” is better).

The source column matters most: Statements = computed from the four stored fiscal years. Master data (TTM) = a ready-made Yahoo figure for the trailing twelve months – more current, but it mixes quarters across the reporting date. Statements + TTM = computed from the statements with the TTM figure as a fallback. Market + statements = a market figure against a balance-sheet figure – these are the ones that stay blank when quote and reporting currency differ. Below the table you also find the substitutes used when Yahoo omits a balance-sheet line.

What is deliberately missing

  • No fair value, no price target. Four fiscal years are enough for ratios, trends and comparison – not to anchor a company value. Every common method for that (Graham number, median multiple, DCF) needs either a decade of history or freely chosen assumptions, and then produces numbers that move more with the assumption than with the company.
  • No momentum panel. RSI and price momentum are already on the Trend and Key Data tabs – and without the peers' price history there would be no percentile for them anyway.
  • No PEG. Yahoo fills that field for barely a quarter of the equities.

Limits of the data

  • Four fiscal years – hence a three-year CAGR as the longest growth window. On a negative base (a loss year) the CAGR is left blank rather than producing a bogus figure.
  • Banks and insurers: Altman Z, the liquidity ratios, working-capital metrics and all EV multiples stay blank – there “debt” is funding and enterprise value is not a sensible numerator.
  • Currency: price and accounts can diverge (ADRs, pence listings on the LSE, HKD-quoted mainland companies – about 9 % of equities). Metrics mixing both are converted; without a rate they stay blank rather than computing in mixed units.
  • Piotroski F-Score: seven criteria come from two years of statements, the two cash-flow criteria from the TTM figure in the master data – no per-fiscal-year cash flow statement is stored. Both are level tests, not year-on-year comparisons.
  • Master-data age: the Sunday ASSET_INFO job keeps it fresh to within a week; the date is shown in the header.

Valuation over time

P/E, P/S and P/B are formed daily from the price and the per-share figure of the most recently reported fiscal year – so the ratio steps at every reporting date. The dashed line is the median across these four years: a description of where the valuation has been, not a target.

Operating the price chart

  • Zoom: mouse wheel or drag a selection box
  • Pan: click + drag (after a double-click reset)
  • Camera icon (top right of the chart): screenshot as PNG
  • Plotly toolbar: zoom reset, axis scaling, hide data points
The chart first loads data from the local SQLite database; if newer prices are missing, Yahoo Finance is queried automatically as a fallback.

Band overlays & non-trading times

Several overlays draw filled bands or clouds around the price, e.g.:

  • Bollinger Bands (slow + fast band)
  • Heikin-Ashi EMA high/low band
  • G-FRAMA ATR band
  • DEMA trend fill (green/red)
  • Ichimoku (ICI) Kumo cloud (bullish/bearish)

The x-axis hides non-trading times (weekends, bank holidays and – for intraday – the overnight sessions) so that empty price gaps do not stretch the chart. Because trading hours differ per asset class (equities, FX, bonds, funds, crypto), the filled bands are interrupted at exactly these gaps.

The thin vertical lines / interruptions in the bands are not a rendering error – they mark non-trading times, weekends or bank holidays when no prices exist for that asset. The edge and mid lines of the bands run through continuously. Continuously tradable instruments (e.g. crypto, 24/7) show a gap-free band.

In the ⚙ Settings this can be toggled via "Mark non-trading times in bands" (mark_nontrading_times):

  • Yes (default) – bands are interrupted at non-trading times (gaps as markers). Always displayed correctly.
  • No – continuous, smoother fill without markers.

Buy/sell signals in the chart

  • ▲ green – buy signal (overallValueTrend crosses the threshold)
  • ▼ red – sell signal (EWO below EMA or OVT falls below the threshold)

Regime / market-stress banner

If the asset belongs to a real stock index (ticker with ^), an early-warning banner about the index's market breadth can appear below the chart: how the share of bull/bear/sideways index members is shifting, plus a divergence term (index holds, breadth crumbles). It is a probability-based early-warning signal with a days-to-weeks lead time, not a crash prediction.

  • Hidden by default — enable it in the ⚙ Settings via "Show regime / market-stress banner" (show_regime).
  • The initial computation of large indices (e.g. ^RUT, ~1900 members) takes a while; the result is then cached per day. The scheduler job WARM_MARKET_STRESS can warm the cache in the morning so the first page load does not wait.
