Fear & Greed Index fear_greed_page.py

The Fear & Greed Index condenses seven market signals into a single value from 0 to 100. It measures not price direction but the sentiment behind it: 0 = extreme fear (panic selling, cheap valuations), 100 = extreme greed (complacency, overbought markets). Meant as a contrarian compass – extremes often mark turning points.

RangeReading
0–24Extreme Fear
25–44Fear
45–55Neutral
56–75Greed
76–100Extreme Greed

The seven components & their sources

ComponentWhat it measuresData source
MomentumIndex above/below its SMA125 – normalised against its own history. Far above the average = greed.yf_<index> (daily close)
BreadthAverage of % of members above SMA200, % with RSI > 50 and the bullish share (Markov regime). Broad participation = greed.asset_simulation (all index members)
52-week strengthNet new 52-week highs minus lows (percentile-normalised against its own multi-year history). asset_simulation (lyHigh/lyLow)
VolatilityVIX relative to its 1-year range. High VIX = fear (inverted).yf_^VIX
Term structureVIX vs. VIX3M. When the curve tips into backwardation (short-term dearer than 3M), fear rises. Substitutes the classic put/call ratio, which is not available from the data source.yf_^VIX, yf_^VIX3M
Safe haven20-day return of the index minus TLT (Treasuries). Stocks beating bonds = greed.yf_<index>, yf_TLT
Junk-bond demand20-day return of HYG (high yield) minus LQD (investment grade). Tight credit spreads = risk appetite.yf_HYG, yf_LQD

How the value is built

  • Every component is normalised against its own multi-year history (percentile or z-score → 0–100). This matters for breadth: 71 % above SMA200 sounds greedy but is normal in a bull market – only the comparison with history (including the 2020/2022 bear markets, ~6.5 years) turns it into a sentiment signal (e.g. 71 % → 68th percentile → 68 points).
  • The breadth history comes from the yearly databases asset_simulation_{YEAR}.db (2020–last year) plus the current year from _all.db – the latter alone only holds the current year and would read every uptrend as "greed".
  • The composite is the equal-weighted mean of all available components. If a series is missing, the average is taken over the rest.

Usage

  • Index selector: breadth is computed locally for the chosen index (^SPX, ^GDAXI, ^FTSE, …).
  • Gauge: the composite with a fear → greed colour scale.
  • Component bars: each sub-value with a short rationale (e.g. "VIX 18.2 (64th percentile)").
  • 🔄 Refresh: forces a recompute (otherwise cached per day).
Breadth is local to each index; the macro series (VIX, bonds, credit) are US/global and serve as a risk backdrop – even for a European or Asian index.
The index is a sentiment gauge, not a buy or sell signal. If a macro series (e.g. VIX3M) is not loaded up to date, the index uses the latest available value – a routine data fetch keeps it fresh.
