Fear & Greed Index fear_greed_page.py

The Fear & Greed Index condenses seven market signals into a single value from 0 to 100. It measures not price direction but the sentiment behind it: 0 = extreme fear (panic selling, cheap valuations), 100 = extreme greed (complacency, overbought markets). Meant as a contrarian compass – extremes often mark turning points.

How the value is built

  • Every component is normalised against its own multi-year history (percentile or z-score → 0–100). This matters for breadth: 71 % above SMA200 sounds greedy but is normal in a bull market – only the comparison with history (including the 2020/2022 bear markets, ~6.5 years) turns it into a sentiment signal (e.g. 71 % → 68th percentile → 68 points).
  • The breadth history comes from the yearly databases asset_simulation_{YEAR}.db (2020–last year) plus the current year from _all.db – the latter alone only holds the current year and would read every uptrend as "greed".
  • The composite is the equal-weighted mean of all available components. If a series is missing, the average is taken over the rest.

Usage

  • Index selector: breadth is computed locally for the chosen index (^SPX, ^GDAXI, ^FTSE, …).
  • Gauge: the composite with a fear → greed colour scale.
  • Component bars: each sub-value with a short rationale (e.g. "VIX 18.2 (64th percentile)").
  • 🔄 Refresh: forces a recompute (otherwise cached per day).

How the components work

The seven building blocks measure very different things; only the shared normalisation makes them additive. Each component is translated into a 0–100 percentile rank against its own multi-year history — not against a fixed scale. "VIX 18" on its own is meaningless; what matters is where 18 sits in the distribution of the last years. That keeps the index comparable across market regimes.

How the overall value is formed

The index is the equal-weighted mean of the available components. If a series is missing (e.g. because VIX3M is not meaningful for an index) it is dropped rather than counted as 0 — which is why the line below the gauge states how many building blocks went in. Fewer blocks does not mean "wrong", but more narrowly supported.

How to read it

  • Extremes are contrarian. Great greed clusters at tops, great fear at bottoms. The index is a sentiment gauge, not a trend follower — it does not say "buy", it says "sentiment is one-sided".
  • Direction beats level. A fall from 80 to 60 is a different signal from a rise from 40 to 60, although both end at 60. That is why the change over roughly 30 days is shown next to the value.
  • Mid-range values say little. Between about 45 and 55 the statement is weak; there the individual bars are more informative than the total.
  • Divergences are the most interesting part. Rising price with falling breadth means the advance is carried by fewer and fewer names.

Limitations

  • The macro series (VIX, TLT, HYG, LQD) are US data. For a DAX or Nikkei index only breadth is local; the rest remains a global risk statement.
  • Percentile normalisation needs history. Shortly after adding an index the values are unstable.
  • The index can stay at an extreme for a long time. "Extreme greed" is a state, not a moment — as a standalone trigger it is unsuitable.
Breadth is local to each index; the macro series (VIX, bonds, credit) are US/global and serve as a risk backdrop – even for a European or Asian index.
The index is a sentiment gauge, not a buy or sell signal. If a macro series (e.g. VIX3M) is not loaded up to date, the index uses the latest available value – a routine data fetch keeps it fresh.
