Risk profile risk_profile_page.py · risk_profile.py · indicator/prof.py

The page answers one question: how much movement are you willing to sit through? The answer cuts the universe down, and only inside that cut does the ranking by trend happen. That order is not taste, it is measured — see below.

Why the profile sits on the risk axis

Over 2020-2026 (5.02m rows of asset_simulation) return is barely predictable: the backtest's composite score reaches a rank correlation of 0.065. Risk on the other hand is highly predictable: 0.70 for future volatility, -0.33 for drawdown.

Hence the design: a profile makes a promise about volatility and drawdown — one that can be kept and checked. A promise about return could not be kept, so the page does not make one.

The cut runs on the average daily move (ATR relative to price). It predicts future volatility marginally better than logVola (0.71 vs 0.68) and it is the one number a person can picture: "this thing moves about 2 % on an average day". Where ATR is missing, logVola stands in, cut at the same coverage.

The band is measured, not asserted

"Conservative" does not mean "never more than 25 % volatility". It means: half the time below 21 %, and 85 % of the time below 32 %. Every number carries its quantile, because a band without one is not a promise but a wish.

Calibration ran over 2020-2026 including the covid crash — calibrating on calm years alone would have set the bands too tight. Cross-check: calibrated on 2020-2023, measured on 2024-2026. The volatility bands held in 83-86 % of cases (target 85), the drawdown floor in 90-94 % (target 90). The promise therefore survives outside its calibration window.

Calm costs return

Every profile trails the universe: the conservative one by 0.98 percentage points per 21 days, most clearly in 2020 at 5.5 points — after the crash, quiet names lagged the recovery. A profile is judged on whether it keeps its promise, not on whether it beats the market.

The trend score

trendScore measures how close a name is to its own record high (0 = a full round trip down, 100 = at the high). Nine trend components and ten combinations were tested — momentum over 1, 6 and 12 months, distance to the moving averages, the slope of the 200-day average, and blends of those.

None of them predicts return reliably. The highest average return in fact came from the names furthest below their high (1.93 % vs 0.85 % per 21 days) — mean reversion, flattered by a universe that only contains the survivors.

What holds up is consistency. Across the five quintiles of distance to the high:

In Strategy Finder and Multi Strategies

The columns riskScore, riskBucket and trendScore exist live in the chart and in asset_simulation under the same names. The expression at the bottom of the page copies straight into a buy formula:

(atr / close <= 0.03) & (trendScore >= 75)

0 means "not computed yet", not "step 0". New columns are created with a default of 0. riskBucket <= 2 would pull in every unfilled row — write (riskBucket >= 1) & (riskBucket <= 2) instead.
Fixed thresholds depend on the market. Because the calibration is absolute, trendScore >= 75 returns fewer names in a weak market than in a strong one. That is intended — it is what makes the chart show the same number as the backtest — but it has to be planned for in the number of positions.

Position size

Optional — switch it on under fine tuning. Instead of spreading the budget over the day's selection, every position then carries the profile's target volatility:

budget = capital / number of positions x (target vol / the name's vol), clamped to at most twice and at least half an equal slot.

A name whose swing matches the target gets exactly one slot, a calmer one more, a livelier one less. The decisive difference to the existing spread: relative weighting always spends the whole budget, whatever happens to be on offer that day — so a day of nothing but restless names quietly builds a restless portfolio. The absolute rule buys less on such a day.

Capital deployed is therefore usually below 100 %. On today's universe it averages around 85 %. That is not a fault but the promise: only as much risk is taken as the profile states. If you want the full capital invested, leave the switch off.

The same formula applies in three places — Strategy Finder and Multi Strategies via sizing_cap = profile, the signals tab and the agent via the logged-in user's profile. The three are tied together by tests, because they have drifted apart before.

Maintenance

  • Fill the columns for existing data: python asset_perf2.py /backfill:prof /force (add /year:2024 for an older year). The /force is mandatory.
  • Re-measure the bands: python -m tradinglib.risk_profile --calibrate, add --save to store them app-wide.
  • Test a building block against the universe: python score_eval.py.

Moving a profile's cut by hand makes the displayed band inaccurate — re-calibrate afterwards. The measured fields themselves are deliberately not editable.
