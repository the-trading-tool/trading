Global Rotation global_rotation_page.py

Shows where capital rotates between the major markets — as a relative-strength proxy. Actual fund flows (EPFR, ETF creation/redemption) are not in the data; relative strength is the established substitute.

Preparation

  • Each market index is converted to EUR via its FX pair — the currency move itself is part of the capital flow (e.g. EUR appreciation = inflow into EUR assets).
  • The benchmark is an equal-weighted EUR basket of the markets themselves (no external ticker).
  • Sources: index daily prices (yf_^…), FX pairs ({CCY}EUR=X, EURUSD=X, GBPUSD=X). Markets without an available FX series are skipped.

The metrics — how they are computed and what they say

Why everything is computed in EUR

Every index is converted into EUR via its FX pair before relative strength is derived. This is not cosmetic: for an investor with a EUR account the currency move is part of the return. A market can rise in local currency and still lose in EUR — the page shows the view that matters for the portfolio.

The benchmark is the basket itself

The reference is an equal-weighted EUR basket of the markets shown, not an external index. Every statement is therefore explicitly relative within the selection: "leading" means better than the average of the other markets, not better than the world. If all markets fall, the basket falls too and a market can lead while losing money.

RS-Ratio and RS-Momentum

With RS = market / basket (both EUR-normalised and rebased to a common start):

  • RS-Ratio = 100 + (EMA10(RS) / EMA40(RS) − 1) × 100 — the level: >100 = the market beats the basket.
  • RS-Momentum = 100 + (RS-Ratio / EMA5(RS-Ratio) − 1) × 100 — the change of that level.

Because the second is the derivative of the first, markets rotate clockwise through the quadrants. Improving marks the early inflow, Weakening the early outflow — both turn before the level changes.

Mansfield RSC — the in-/outflow ranking

RSC = (RS / 30-week moving average(RS) − 1) × 100, the same definition as in sector rotation. It does not answer "who is strong?" but "where is something shifting relative to its own normal?" — which is what makes it usable as a flow proxy.

Pair matrix

Each cell is the difference between the EUR returns of two markets over the chosen window (in percentage points). The matrix is antisymmetric: a cell's mirror carries the same number with the opposite sign. Useful for the concrete question "US or Europe?", which the RRG alone does not answer.

Important: this is a proxy via relative strength, not measured fund flows. It is derived from prices, not from fund data — strong relative performance implies inflow but does not prove it.
The metrics are identical to sector rotation (JdK RS-Ratio/RS-Momentum, Mansfield RSC) — here applied to market indices instead of sector ETFs.
Relative strength is a proxy, not a buy/sell signal. Outperformance can stem from earnings rather than flows; exact directed amounts are not measurable.
