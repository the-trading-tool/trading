Normalised price paths – open positions

For the open positions there are three normalised charts. "Normalised" means: every price is set to a starting value of 100, so that names of different prices become directly comparable. What matters is when this 100 is set – each choice answers a different question.

ChartBase 100X-axisAnswers
Normalised Price Chartcommon start date (selectable at the top)calendar date"Which of my names performed best in the selected window?" – a true peer comparison
Since entry (calendar)each position's own buy daycalendar date"How does each of my positions stand since entry?" – matches the unrealised P&L per position
Since entry (by holding period)each position's own buy daytrading days since purchase (0, 1, 2 …)"Which position develops better than the peers since entry?" – a path comparison independent of the purchase date
Break-even line: In the two "since entry" charts the dotted line at 100 marks your cost basis. A line above 100 = position in profit, below 100 = at a loss.

Why three views? For a pure peer comparison the first chart is the right tool, because all names share the same base and the same window. The two "since entry" charts instead show your actual position performance from the buy day onward:

  • Calendar variant – the lines start on different calendar days (= your respective buy dates). Directly readable: the current profit/loss per position.
  • Holding-period variant – all lines start on the left at 100 and are aligned by "days since purchase". This lets you read, for example, which position ran better 30 trading days after entry than the others – regardless of when you bought it.
The buy date per name is the earliest purchase of the position. So with follow-up buys the original entry remains the reference point. Names without local price data do not appear in the chart.

Position Sizing Calculator

This calculator answers a single question: "How many shares may I buy so that I do not lose more on this trade than I am willing to risk?" So it does not determine how much money you invest, but how large your position may be so that your maximum loss (if the stop-loss triggers) does not exceed a fixed percentage of your account.

Inputs

FieldMeaning
Account sizeYour total trading capital
Risk per trade (%)What percentage of the account you are willing to lose at most on this trade (rule of thumb: 1–2 %)
Entry pricePlanned buy price per share
Stop-loss pricePrice at which you close the position at a loss (must be below the entry)
Take-profit (optional)Target price for exiting with a profit – only for the reward-to-risk ratio

The calculation (example)

Example: account €100,000, risk 2 %, entry €84, stop-loss €82.

StepFormulaExample value
Risk per shareEntry − stop-loss84 − 82 = €2
Risk amount (account)Account size × risk %100,000 × 2 % = €2,000
Recommended share countRisk amount ÷ risk per share2,000 ÷ 2 = 1,000 shares
Position valueShare count × entry1,000 × 84 = €84,000
Important: The position value (€84,000) is only the capital tied up, not your risk. Your actual loss risk remains the risk amount (€2,000) – the real safety net is the stop-loss.

Core idea: The stop-loss distance controls the position size. A tight stop-loss (here €2 distance) allows a large position; a deeper stop-loss (e.g. €79 = €5 distance) allows only 400 shares (2,000 ÷ 5) – the account risk stays identical at €2,000 in both cases.

Take-profit: If you enter a take-profit above the entry, the calculator additionally shows the reward-to-risk ratio. Example take-profit €90 → gain €6 per share ÷ risk €2 = 1 : 3 (you risk €1 to gain €3; rule of thumb: at least 1 : 2).

Trailing stop management

A stop that follows the price up: it moves with the highest price the position has reached since entry and stays put when the price falls. The distance follows the name's own volatility (ATR) rather than a fixed percentage — a quiet stock gets a tight stop, a volatile one a wide stop.

StepComputation
High-water mark (HWM)max(stored HWM, current price) · seeded with the entry price
Stop levelHWM − multiplier × ATR · multiplier adjustable, default 2.5
Without ATRHWM × 0.92 (8 % distance) — fallback when no ATR exists for the name
Breachedcurrent price ≤ stop level → the row is flagged red

The ATR comes from asset_simulation_*.db (most recent stored value), the prices from the local daily series. State is kept per name in trades.db → own_trades_trails.

The HWM only grows when you press "Update trailing stops". There is no scheduled job for it. Highs that occur between two clicks are invisible to the mark, leaving the stop too low. If you rely on the stop, update it regularly.
Every amount is in the system currency. Entry, price, HWM, stop and ATR are brought onto the same currency before anything is computed — the "Currency" column shows which. This is needed because entries in trades.db are booked in the settlement currency (Scalable books EUR) while prices and ATR are in the listing currency: pence for an LSE name, USD in New York. If no exchange rate can be resolved for a name, it is skipped and reported rather than shown with a stop in the wrong currency.
The panel is purely informational — it flags a breached stop but sells nothing. For the automated variant with broker execution (paper/live) see the check_stoploss.py help page.

Period filter

The year dropdown at the top right restricts the analysis to a specific calendar year. By default the current year is shown.

The data lives in database/trades{year}.db. Paper trading uses the same tables as live trading, but with the mode flag paper.
