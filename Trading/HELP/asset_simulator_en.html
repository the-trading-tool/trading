Strategy Finder premium/asset_simulator.pyPremium

An interactive backtest calculator: define your own buy/sell conditions, simulate them on historical data and view performance, trades and portfolio development. It lets you develop new strategies before putting them into production in "Multi-Strategies".

Premium feature (Sidebar → Portfolio → Strategy Finder), visible only to admin accounts with the license feature strategy_engine. Without a license the message "Strategy Finder not available – please check your license" appears.

Simulation parameters

  • Number of assets: maximum number of simultaneously held positions
  • Investment: starting capital of the simulation
  • Buy delay (days): number of days between the signal and the actual entry
  • Fee per trade (%): accounted for in the profit calculation
  • Buy assets when / sell when: free expressions over indicator columns (same syntax as on the Asset overview), e.g. ewo > ewo_ema or ewo < ewo_ema
  • 🧩 For Multi Strategies: expandable below the ISIN switch. Builds the ready entry for the selected index from the formulas and portfolio settings (count, start capital, sort order, signal window, stops, position sizing, fractional shares, ISIN filter, holding period, cooldown, currency) – with a strategy name, to paste under Settings ▸ Strategies & Trading ▸ Transactions. Multi Strategies runs without fee and buy delay and has no sector, exchange, price or size filter, so results differ
    • expandable below the two formula fields. At the top twelve example formulas (trend channel, pullback, crossover with .shift(1), breakout, multi-line with signal window, ATR filter, risk profile, 4PS, market breadth, seasonality …) with an explanation and a "Put into the formula fields" button – patterns for the syntax, not tested strategies. Below that every column the loaded run knows, grouped by indicator with a short description, a search box and a syntax note. Columns marked ⚠ (today's fundamentals, analyst targets, macd_trend_wk) carry information from after the bar and make backtests look better than they would live
Optimisation: If %%varname is used as a placeholder in an expression, the built-in optimiser automatically searches for the best numeric value for that variable.
Optimise with test period: Without this option the optimiser picks the best combination on the same data it reports on – the top result is then often just fitted to the data. With a test period the values are chosen before the selected date only (train%) and measured after it (test%). The rank correlation shows whether the ranking carries; if the best training combination is only mid-field in the test period, it is probably chance. The neighbourhood heatmap shows whether good values form a broad plateau (robust) or a single spike (chance).

Usage

  • Compute starts the simulation with the chosen filters and conditions – with multiple years the data is loaded per year and merged
  • Reset discards the result again
  • If no simulation database exists yet for a chosen year, the computation can be triggered directly via ▶ Start computation (runs in the background, status via ↺ Check status)

Result

  • Full dataset: all simulated rows, with 📥 Excel export
  • Portfolio: development of the simulated positions, "Still in portfolio" list of currently held names
  • Cumulative gain: chart across all trades, with 📥 trade export
  • Metrics: total capital, invested amount, total value, total profit (incl. unrealised gains), win rate, number of trades, implied volatility
  • Monthly performance heatmap and compact KPI overview (analogous to Dashboard/Multi-Strategies)
  • Parity analysis: compares the strategy with a simple "buy once and rebalance by volatility" approach over the same period

🧪 Robustness

Computed on the daily mark-to-market equity (open positions at the daily close), so paper losses between buy and sell are visible too.

  • Comparison: strategy vs the index (buy and hold) and an equally weighted universe of the same names – total, p.a., max drawdown, return/vol
  • Avg. invested and p.a. per euro deployed: a strategy that often sits in cash must be judged on the capital actually deployed
  • Results per year with buys, win rate and drawdown – if a single year carries almost everything, be careful
  • Training and test period: splits the result at a date. Only meaningful if the formula was not chosen with the test period in view – the real check is the optimiser with a test period
  • Trades by market breadth at entry: win rate and average result per breadth range. Market breadth is also available in formulas: mkt_breadth50 / mkt_breadth200 (share of index members above their 50/200-day average, 0–100) and mkt_breadth_chg (change of mkt_breadth50 over 20 days), e.g. (mkt_breadth50 <= 25). Measured 2020–2026, an oversold market was the better entry and a broad advance the worse one – on today's index members (survivorship bias). Also usable is fg_score, the Fear & Greed score (0–100) of the index the name belongs to (S&P 500, Russell 2000, DAX, MDAX, SDAX, Nikkei, FTSE, IBEX, SMI). Fear (fg_score < 45) was the better entry in all 7 years, even without washed-out breadth and then at normal volatility; greed (≥ 75) the worse one. Note: the score has 3 components in 2020–21, 5 in 2022–24 and 7 only since 2025.
  • Random-order comparison: the same signals, but taken each day in random order into the free slots. If the own run is not clearly above (percentile below 75), the ranking adds nothing or the result is noise

🧱 Strategy building blocks

By default the Strategy Finder works with building blocks. Settings ▸ Strategies & Trading ▸ Strategy Finder switches back to the classic sidebar. With buy formula / highest value / sell formula both modes compute exactly the same.

  • 📇 Strategy (top): name, idea in one sentence and status (idea / paper / live). Save stores every setting – index, filters, years, formulas, blocks, slots, size, fee, stops, periods – and, if nothing changed since, the key figures of the last run and the signal-check lights. Load puts everything back; entries from Multi Strategies (🧩) can be loaded too. Below, the strategy in plain words.
  • Universe of several indices: the index field takes several indices; their stocks then compete for the same slots. A stock in several indices counts once. All stocks must trade in one currency – the simulator does not convert. A bundle that mixes markets (a second currency above 5 % of the stocks) is blocked; the currency filter narrows it to one currency. Single strays (e.g. a stock with a US listing in the MDAX) are named and computed as in the single index. The "index (hold)" comparison is dropped for a bundle; the equal-weight universe stays. In Multi Strategies a saved bundle becomes one entry with the key '^GDAXI + ^MDAXI' – one pot, the same shared slots as here, live as well.
  • Entry: buy formula · always (every stock is a candidate every day) · random (a share of stock-days, with a seed – same seed, same result)
  • Selection for free slots: highest or lowest value of the "order by" column · an expression of same-day columns (e.g. sortino / vola) · random
  • Exit: sell formula · formula or after N calendar days at the latest · after N days only · never (stops only)
  • 📈 History / 📊 Compare all (on the card): every "Calculate" run of a saved strategy is recorded – total, p.a., drawdown, return/vol, avg. invested, against the equally weighted market, trades, plus the signal-check lights and the matched-random percentile once they were run. The same settings on the same day are one line. "Compare all" lines up every saved strategy by its latest run of the saved settings. Runs are kept in strategy_reports.db, at most 200 per strategy.
  • Matched random (in Robustness): random stocks with the strategy's signal density and median holding period, otherwise the same settings. The fair yardstick: if the strategy is not clearly above it (percentile below 90), slots, holding period and market deliver the result, not the formula.
Loading from Multi Strategies sets fee and buy delay to 0, because Multi Strategies computes that way; missing fields come from the global settings. As when changed by hand, position size, fractional shares, periods, signal window and ISIN filter are stored globally – Multi Strategies reads these values too. A saved strategy goes to Multi Strategies as a reference ("🧩 For Multi Strategies as a reference") – with every block and for bundles too. Written out (formulas instead of a reference) only works with buy formula, "highest value" selection, sell formula and a single index.

🔬 Signal check

Checks the formula itself, without the portfolio's slot mechanics. A good portfolio result can come from the order in which signals meet free slots, or from a few long-held names – the signal check shows whether the formula knows anything underneath. Both parts run only on a button.

  • Buy signals: return after each new buy signal (from the buy delay on) over 21 and 63 trading days, minus the equally weighted market over the same days. No slot limit, every signal counts
  • Sell signals: the same after a sell signal. A good exit is followed by weaker weeks (negative); positive means it sells winners too early
  • Control with random entries: your entries and random entries in the same names, both closed by your sell rule. A sell rule that only sells into strength makes almost any entry look good – the difference is what the buy rule adds
  • Concentration: share of the run's net gain from its best 3 and 5 trades
  • Other markets: the same formula and settings in other indices (without the sector, exchange, currency, size and price filters), each against its equally weighted market. Large indices take up to a minute
The lights are rules of thumb; the thresholds are shown below the results. Signals and control are computed without fees, other markets with. The universe is always today's index membership (survivorship bias).
Since 2026-09-21 the total profit includes realised gains of earlier round trips in a name that is held again at the end. Before, they were dropped (^GDAXI 2024–25: 50.2 % instead of 61.2 %). Older results are therefore too low.
Multi-year simulations and large indices can take a few seconds to minutes, depending on the data volume. duckdb speeds up the queries but is optional.
