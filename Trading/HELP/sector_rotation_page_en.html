Choosing the universe

At the top of the dashboard: US (S&P 500 sectors), EU (STOXX 600 sectors) or EM (Emerging Markets). The chosen universe determines the sectors, ETFs and reference index.

The metrics — how they are computed and what they say

All four measure relative strength: not whether a sector rises, but whether it does better than its reference index. A sector can fall and still lead, if the market falls harder.

RSC — Mansfield relative strength

RS = sector ETF / benchmark (weekly closes), then RSC = (RS / 30-week moving average(RS) − 1) × 100.

The trick is the moving average in the denominator: what is measured is not the outperformance itself but its deviation from its own trend. A sector that has outperformed steadily for years hovers around 0 — it only stands out when the outperformance accelerates or fades. Positive = the sector is doing better than it typically did over the last 30 weeks. The Δ next to it is the week-over-week change, i.e. the direction.

RRG — RS-Ratio and RS-Momentum

The Relative Rotation Graph uses the same ratio series, but smoothed:

AxisFormulaMeaning
RS-Ratio (x) 100 + (EMA10(RS) / EMA40(RS) − 1) × 100 Level of relative strength. >100 = ahead of the benchmark
RS-Momentum (y) 100 + (RS-Ratio / EMA5(RS-Ratio) − 1) × 100 Change in relative strength — the derivative of the x axis

Because the y axis is the change of the x axis, sectors typically travel clockwise: momentum turns before the level follows. That is why Improving (top left) is the early entry quadrant and Weakening (bottom right) the early warning — not Lagging. The tail shows the last weeks (days in the daily tab); its length is the speed of rotation, its direction matters more than the snapshot.

CMF — Chaikin Money Flow (14 days)

Per bar: MFM = ((C − L) − (H − C)) / (H − L), weighted by volume, then CMF = Σ₁₄(MFM × volume) / Σ₁₄(volume).

The multiplier asks: where in the day's range did price close? Near the high → +1 (buyers kept control), near the low → −1. Multiplied by volume this becomes a measure of capital in- or outflow. Positive = accumulation, negative = distribution. Unlike price, CMF captures how firmly a move is backed — a rising price with negative CMF is a poorly supported advance.

OBV trend — slope of On-Balance Volume (20 days)

OBV = cumulative sum(sign(Δclose) × volume); a linear regression over the last 20 days, its slope divided by average volume and scaled by 100.

OBV adds the full day's volume when the close is up and subtracts it when down. The absolute value is meaningless; the slope is the statement: positive = net volume flowing in. Normalising by average volume makes ETFs of different size comparable — without it XLK alone would dominate every smaller sector.

Read together: RSC and RRG say whether a sector is relatively strong, CMF and OBV say whether capital confirms it. Leading in the RRG with negative CMF means the lead is no longer carried by inflows — a candidate for the move into Weakening.

Limitations

  • Every value is relative to the chosen benchmark. Switching the universe (US / EU / EM) changes the benchmark and therefore every number.
  • RSC works on weekly closes and needs 30 weeks of history; young ETFs do not appear.
  • CMF and OBV need reliable volume. For European ETFs it is fragmented across venues — those values are coarser than for the US SPDRs.
  • The RRG tail is smoothed (EMA); it lags deliberately and turns later than price after a shock.
