Withdrawal Simulator withdrawal_simulation.py

Simulates spending down capital in retirement over a whole lifetime, using your own depot, additionally selected ETFs and commodities, and a fixed-deposit share. The result is not a single curve but the probability that the money lasts a lifetime.

What every simulation computes (month by month)

ComponentModel
MarketsBlock bootstrap: 12-month blocks from the real history (default 20 years) of all assets at once. Correlations, crashes and recoveries are kept. Returns in the system currency incl. FX effects.
Wealth todayDepot from “Own Transactions” (values editable) plus fixed/overnight deposits as a table of bank, amount and rate (can be saved). Total wealth = sum; the simulation uses the amount-weighted average rate or, optionally, inflation + spread.
Target splitDeposit share of total wealth (1 % steps), the rest by stocks %, ETFs %, commodities %. Every asset has a class (suggested, changeable) and a weight within its class. Sales from the depot when switching cost flat-rate tax on the gain share (after the saver's allowance), deducted from the starting capital.
ScenariosCurrent split (depot and deposits as today) against target split; optionally “target ETFs / commodities only”. All on the same random paths.
Withdrawal modelFixed (planned amount, inflation-adjusted) or dynamic, adjusted once a year and limited by floor/ceiling: guardrails (Guyton-Klinger: withdrawal rate 20 % above/below the start → cut/raise 10 %), fixed percentage of current wealth (in 0.1 % steps), by expected volatility (calm 12 months → more, rough or drawdown > 10 % → less; raises only above the real starting capital), skip inflation raise after loss years. A comparison of all models on the same random paths shows success, actual spending, lowest spending year and estate.
One-off expensesTable with year, purpose, amount (today's money) and optional repetition (“every 8 years until 2045”). Paid in January of the year on top of regular spending, inflation-adjusted, with tax on gains sold, not cut by the withdrawal model; marked purple in the wealth chart. Saved automatically.
InflationConstant or fluctuating (AR(1): yearly shocks that persist, producing spikes like 2022). Spending, pensions and care costs grow with each path's price level. Results in today's money.
Taxes & costsTax on capital income per person and calendar year, as brackets with allowance and partial exemption (all adjustable, can be saved as default). Presets: Germany 26.375 % (+ church tax 8/9 %), saver's allowance €1,000 per person, 30 % partial exemption for equity ETFs; Spain (savings base) 19 % up to €6,000, 21 % up to €50,000, 23 % up to €200,000, 27 % up to €300,000, 30 % above, no allowance, no partial exemption, nominal brackets. Taxed are the gain share of every sale, interest and dividends; for a couple it is split between both. Not included: Spanish wealth tax, tax-free traspasos. Plus running costs (TER).
Wealth tax (Spain)Optional with tax “Spain”: Impuesto sobre el Patrimonio once a year on net wealth per person (depot + deposits + further wealth, halved for a couple), state scale 0.2–3.5 % above the exemption (default €700,000), regional rebate (e.g. 100 % Madrid/Andalusia), plus the solidarity tax on wealth above €3 M (1.7/2.1/3.5 %, wealth tax credited). Paid from the portfolio like an expense. With the 60 % cap (límite conjunto): wealth plus income tax at most 60 % of income (gross income per person + interest, without long-term gains), wealth tax reduced by at most 80 %. Income-tax scale selectable: average or Canary Islands (state + Canary half 2025, 18.5–50.5 %). Not included: main-home exemption.
Life expectancyGompertz-Makeham mortality calibrated to the Destatis period life table 2023/2025 (at 65: men 18.0, women 21.1 years), plus a cohort effect (mortality keeps falling, default 1 % p.a.).
Health & lifestyleRisk factor from health, smoking, exercise, weight and alcohol (guide values from cohort studies), fading in old age.
Care riskYearly probability of a paid care case (0.25 % at 60 to about 8 % at 90), monthly co-payment, cost growth above inflation, higher mortality in care.
CoupleTwo life paths; after the first death spending drops (default 70 %) and the survivor receives the widow's pension (default 55 %).

Result

  • Money lasts a lifetime – share of paths without ruin while anyone in the household is alive.
  • Estate, real – wealth at the last death in today's money (median and worst 10 %).
  • Fan chart – wealth distribution by age, with the share of households alive and still solvent.
  • Sustainable spending – highest total monthly spending at, for example, ≤ 5 % ruin risk; “Run with …” takes the amount and reruns all figures for it. At this limit the estate is small.
  • How to read the result – explanation per scenario from its numbers: spending and withdrawal model, pension share, net in/outflow, success, estate (median and “in 90 % of cases at least …”), lowest spending year, care, wealth tax, deposit share.

Asset classes over the lifetime

  • Growth per class – what 1 € in stocks, ETFs, commodities and deposits grows to, on the same random paths, after costs, before taxes, without withdrawals (median, optionally with the 10–90 % range).
  • Wealth split in € – stacked per class over age (median of households alive), either with inflows and outflows (pensions in, spending out) or starting capital only (same paths, without pensions, spending and care costs) or as a comparison with both totals as lines. If “with” is above “starting capital only”, the household pays in on balance (pensions above spending); a hint shows the amount per year.

Crisis stress test

The normal run draws random 12-month blocks from the last (default) 20 years. Earlier crises do not occur in it, and long bear markets are cut into pieces. The stress test therefore inserts a crisis as a fixed sequence of months into every simulated life – right away, from a given year or at random within the first N years – together with the inflation of that time. Before and after it the random path continues, on the same paths as the normal run.

CrisisSource

Great Depression 1929–32, oil crisis 1973–74, dotcom 2000–02rounded calendar-year values: S&P 500 with dividends for stocks and ETFs, gold for commodities, US inflation; in US dollars, spread evenly over the months (the low inside a year is missing)
Currency cut (like 1948)stylised: deposits lose 93.5 % once, securities keep their value, one year of 10 % inflation
Own crisisduration, change of stocks/ETFs and commodities, loss of deposits and inflation freely chosen

With the recovery afterwards appends the 12 months or the year after the crisis as they were then. Without it the path continues at random after the low – the harder test. The table shows per crisis the low of the securities, the success rate and the difference to the normal run; the chart compares the median and the worst 10 % of wealth.

Pensions continue normally in all crises and follow inflation – in the 1930–32 deflation spending and pensions therefore also fall in nominal terms. A fixed deposit rate gains in real terms during deflation; bank failures can only be modelled with the own crisis.

Limits

The past is no promise. Single stocks with high past returns are therefore capped at 8 % p.a. by default. Commodity futures are front-month prices without roll costs. Taxes on the yearly rebalancing within securities, health-insurance contributions on capital income and inheritance tax are not modelled. Life expectancy and care risk are averages, not a medical forecast.
